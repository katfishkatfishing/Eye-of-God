I teach the Peloponnesian War on Tuesdays and Thursdays.

They think I am an eccentric old scholar who speaks too softly about Thucydides. None of them notice I watch the corner of the lecture hall where the shadow from the projector hits the molding.

The ang;e is wrong. It has been wrong since 1994, but without the vitreous burn in my skull, I cannot measure the deviation. I bought a surveyor's theodolite last weekend with departmental funds. I set it up on the roof of the library under the guise of an astronomy demonstration. My hands shook so violently I dropped the micrometer pin onto the concrete.

The faculty thinks I am mourning a wife I never had.

They don't understand. They don't understand that I am an ape who was shown the interior mechanism of a chronometer, then thrown back into the dirt with a pocketful of gears that I no longer have the thumbs to assemble.

Unit 07 rests in the steamer trunk at the foot of my bed, wrapped in four wool blankets. It still sweats. When the weather turns humid before a thunderstorm, the brass corner seams weep clear, odorless grease. It knows where it needs to sit.

I sat on the floor tonight for six hours with Chen's manual open. I read the words until the letters dissolved into black scratches. I know it is a key, Chen told me.
