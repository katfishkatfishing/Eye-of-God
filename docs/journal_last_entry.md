My eyesight has begun to deteriorate, but not in the way the optometrist thinks.
He told me I have early-stage cataracts. He offered me a synthetic lens implant. I ran out of the clinic.

Synthetic lens. Plastic. If they seat that inside my orbital ring, the light will never refract correctly again. The residual scars on my retina are all I have left of the aperture. When I squint into the high afternoon sun, I can still see the faint grid etched into my vitreous humor.

The boy, Khallenghar, stayed after the seminar today.

He didn't ask about the Peloponnesian fleet at Syracuse. He was staring at the corner of my desk blotter where ink ran into the felt.

Why was he looking there? No one looks at the blotter.

What did he see? His eyes are clear. Too clear. Cornea untouched. The light passes straight through his head, and it makes me sick. It makes me sick to watch him blink. He blinks so easily. He doesn't know that every time the lid drops, the horizon shifts four thousandths of a degree.

I had to cover the blotter with my grade ledger before he asked.

My chest has that dry, scraping weight again. Like someone emptied an ashtray behind my ribs. The valve isn't pumping blood, it's dragging across grit. Chen had twenty-two days after the clicking started in his sternum. I counted my own pulse against the mantle clock tonight. Sixty one beats, but between the fortieth and the forty first, there is no sound. Just the vacuum. The perihelion.

The closest approach. That was what Chen always called the fourth phase. 'You must greet the fire at its nearest swing or the shutter stays frozen.' 

The trunk under the bed gave a single metallic click at 3:14 AM.

Not a latch opening. A tooth settling deeper into the bone.

How many do I have left? The optometrist said: 'Cover the left, read the lowest line.' I told him there are no lines, only circles that narrow into a throat. He wrote 'zero' on the yellow slip. 

Zero functional receptors. He is wrong. 

The right one still registers the cold. If the circuit board wakes, it won't ask for a name. The machine only recognizes the pupil that stayed open when the roof came down.

Aperture three. Nearest to the sun. It only breathes when the count is zero. 

Everything is crowded. 

The room has five corners tonight.
